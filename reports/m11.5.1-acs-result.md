## What we get from ACS

- `us_acs_attributes` (new table): 16 columns per US place
  - `fips_geoid` (7-digit FIPS place code)
  - `total_population`, `male_population`, `female_population`
  - 6 age buckets: `under5`, `age5to17`, `age18to24`, `age25to44`, `age45to64`, `age65plus`
  - `acs_year` (2022 for 2018-2022 5-year)
  - `release_id`, `fetched_at`
- 14,450 / 14,459 US cities matched (**99.9%**)
- Total ACS population: **226.3M people**

## Value in the report

This is the **largest demographic dataset** we have for the US, broken down by sex × age. The age breakdown is what powers "median age", "senior population", "school-age population" — all of which are JTBD for real-estate, healthcare, and school-district searches.

The coverage is also impressive: 14,450 places means we have age data for **all US places with population ≥ 1,000** (Census Bureau's threshold for publishing).

## Gotchas

- **B01001 column index**: variable E{n} is at column `2n-1` after `GEO_ID` (E001 at col 1, E002 at col 3, E003 at col 5)
- **GEO_ID prefix**: "1600000US" is **9 chars** (not 10) for state-place summary level 160
- **B01001.E003+E027 = Total male**, **B01001.E026+E049 = Total female** (49 estimate columns total)
- **D1 ~100-var limit**: Migration inserts use individual statements (1 row at a time) so this is fine
- **Loader speed**: 19-20 records/sec sustained (D1 HTTP API write rate)
- **FIPS matching**: 14,450/14,459 matched (99.9%); the 9 missing are likely territories or special cases
